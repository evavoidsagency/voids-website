WHERE TO FIND A WORKING STUDENT: CHANNELS FOR EMPLOYERS

Study programmes, job platforms, communities, your own network or an agency: where do you find a working student, and what do you arrange along the way? A sober comparison, including what VOIDS itself offers.

You find a working student through the channels where students already are: the career or internship office of universities of applied sciences and research universities, job platforms, study associations and communities, your own network and recruitment agencies. No channel is always best; it depends on the role, your time and how much you want to do yourself. In 2025/26 more than 770,000 students were enrolled in higher education (preliminary CBS figure) [1], so there's no shortage of candidates.

Which channels are there?

  • Study programmes. For internships at university and applied-sciences level, the government's business portal mentions job platforms, the programme's own internship office and sharing the vacancy on social media [2]. For working-student vacancies the same channels are a logical starting point.
  • Job platforms and social media. You reach many people, but you have to assess responses and schedule interviews yourself.
  • Communities and study associations. A direct route to students who are actively looking. VOIDS reaches more than 4,000 students and starters through a WhatsApp community and a job board; that's the size of the community, not of a talent pool [3].
  • Your own network. Fast and familiar, but the reach is limited to people your network already knows.
  • Recruitment and selection by an agency. The agency searches, selects and presents; you choose and sign the contract. At VOIDS you receive a pre-selected shortlist after the intake [4]. See our article on recruitment and selection, temp agencies or hiring yourself for the differences.

How do you make your vacancy findable and attractive?

Working higher-education students in the third quarter of 2023 most often worked as sales assistants in retail (10%), in hospitality (8%) or as shelf stockers (5%) [5]. A role that fits a degree programme, with concrete tasks, a clear number of hours a week, an hourly rate or range and room around exams, stands out from that. Write about the role, not about the candidate.

What should you watch before someone starts?

  • Check identity and right to work before the first working day. According to UWV, students from outside the EEA with a residence permit for study may work at most 16 hours a week, or full-time in June, July and August, and the employer needs an employment permit for that [6].
  • Put the terms in writing; our article on hiring a working student contains the checklist.
  • Plan guidance and a fixed point of contact for the first month; see our article on onboarding.

What does VOIDS offer?

VOIDS offers two forms: recruitment and selection, and placing your vacancy in our community and on our job board. According to the website, vacancy posting has a fixed price per placement, determined by the reach of the chosen channels, or an ongoing variant at a fixed rate [3]. You receive specific amounts on request.
