HOW TO WRITE A JOB POSTING THAT ACTUALLY SPEAKS TO JUNIOR TALENT

A list of requirements doesn't draw good candidates in, it keeps them out. What does work in a vacancy for working students and starters.

This article is general information, not legal, tax or financial advice. Rules change and your situation may differ: check the current rules with an official source or a qualified advisor before relying on it.

Many vacancies for junior roles read as if they were written for a senior position: a long list of requirements, vague company blurbs and a job title that says little. A text that shows what the work involves and who's behind it works better for someone just starting out.

Why does a long requirements list backfire on junior vacancies?

In a junior role, where limited experience is expected by definition, a long list of requirements is mostly a hurdle, even when those requirements were “nice to have”. Cut what isn't really needed, and add what someone can learn in the role.

What should take the place of a requirements list?

Describe the work itself: what does someone do in a typical week, who do they work with, and what decisions can they make independently after a few months. That's exactly the information a candidate needs to judge whether the role fits, and exactly the information most vacancies skip.

How specific should a vacancy be about pay and hours?

As specific as possible. State a concrete hourly rate or a clear range, and the number of hours a week, instead of “competitive”. The EU pay transparency directive gives applicants the right to receive, before the interview, information about the starting pay or range, for example in a published job ad [1]; the Dutch bill was submitted on 21 May 2026 and still has to be adopted [2]. The salary can't be below the statutory minimum wage [3].

What attracts junior talent, beyond salary?

According to CBS, in 2024 young employees (aged 15 to 27) named good managers, a good salary and job security as the most important working conditions; young people in education more often than others found it important to set their own working hours (84% versus 74%) and to work part-time (81% versus 68%) [4]. What you can translate from that into your vacancy:

  • A clear picture of guidance: who the point of contact is, and what the first month looks like
  • Clarity about hours and flexibility around exams
  • Concrete examples of what a working student in this role does
  • Room to grow within the role, instead of a job that looks exactly the same after a year
  • A tone that matches how the team actually communicates

How long should a good junior vacancy be?

Shorter than you think. Show in a few paragraphs what the work involves, what it pays and who's behind it, instead of a page full of company values and requirements. Put the most important information at the top.
